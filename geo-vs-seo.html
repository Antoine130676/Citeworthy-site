Home / GEO vs SEO

SEO got you ranked.
GEO gets you quoted.

Two different games, played on the same internet. Here's exactly what changes when the reader is an AI model instead of a human scrolling a results page.

The core difference

Search Engine Optimization was built for a world where a human reads a list of ten results and picks one to click. Every tactic — keywords, backlinks, meta descriptions — exists to move your business up that list.

Generative Engine Optimization exists because that list is disappearing. When someone asks ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overview a question, the model reads dozens of sources privately and hands back a short, direct answer — usually citing two or three of them. There's no scrolling. There's no tenth result. You're either one of the few things quoted, or you don't exist in that answer at all.

Why "ranking #1" can still mean invisible

A business can rank first on Google and still be completely absent from AI-generated answers, because the two systems score different things. Google's ranking algorithm weighs backlinks, page speed, and keyword relevance. An AI answer engine weighs something closer to: is this content structured clearly enough, and stated confidently enough, to lift and quote without risk of being wrong?

That means thin, keyword-stuffed pages that still rank on Google often get skipped entirely by AI models — while a smaller competitor with clear, well-structured, direct-answer content gets quoted instead.

What actually changes in practice

  • Structure over keywords. Clear headings, direct answers near the top of the page, and scannable formatting matter more than keyword density.
  • Confidence over persuasion. AI models favor content that states facts plainly. Marketing language and hedging both reduce the odds of being quoted.
  • Entity clarity over backlinks. Schema markup and consistent business details across the web help models understand exactly who you are — this now matters as much as who links to you.
  • Being the source, not the destination. The goal shifts from "get the click" to "get the quote," even when the user never visits your site at all.

Do you still need SEO?

Yes — traditional search still drives real traffic, and a lot of GEO fundamentals (fast pages, clear structure, credible content) support SEO too. The two aren't opposites. But treating GEO as an afterthought means quietly losing the growing share of customers who never see a search results page at all.
